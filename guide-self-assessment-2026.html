Self Assessment in 2026: what actually changes with MTD

2026-08-01 · Finrock team

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD IT) went live in April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000. If that's you, the annual January scramble is being replaced by a steadier rhythm: four quarterly updates plus a tax return at the end of the year.

Who is in scope now

  • From April 2026: self-employment and/or property income over £50,000.
  • From April 2027: the threshold drops to £30,000.
  • Below that, you continue with the normal Self Assessment return — for now.

What a quarterly update actually is

It is not four tax returns. Each update is a summary of income and expenses for the quarter, sent from MTD-compatible software. No tax is due at that point, and mistakes can be corrected in a later update. The tax calculation happens once, when you submit your tax return at the end of the year.

What you need to do differently

  • Keep digital records — a shoebox of receipts no longer satisfies the rules on its own.
  • Use software that can submit updates through HMRC's API.
  • Know your deadlines: updates are due one month and seven days after each quarter ends.

What stays the same

Payments on account, the 31 January balancing payment, and the underlying tax rules are unchanged. MTD changes how you report, not what you owe.
