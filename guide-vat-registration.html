VAT registration: when the £90,000 threshold catches you

2026-06-02 · Finrock team

VAT registration becomes compulsory when your taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 months — not in a tax year, not in a calendar year. That distinction is where most people get caught.

The rolling test

At the end of every month, add up taxable sales for the previous 12 months. If the total exceeds £90,000, you must register within 30 days, and you're registered from the first day of the second month after you went over. Miss it and HMRC can charge the VAT you should have collected — out of your own pocket — plus penalties.

The forward test

There's a second trigger: if you expect to pass £90,000 in the next 30 days alone (a single large contract, say), you must register immediately.

What changes when you register

  • You charge VAT (usually 20%) on sales and issue VAT invoices.
  • You reclaim VAT on most business purchases — including, with limits, some incurred before registration.
  • You file VAT returns digitally each quarter under Making Tax Digital, with boxes 1–9 calculated from digital records.

Worth registering early?

Sometimes. If your customers are VAT-registered businesses, they reclaim what you charge — and you reclaim VAT on your costs. If you sell to the public, registration effectively raises your prices or cuts your margin, so most stay below the threshold as long as they can.
