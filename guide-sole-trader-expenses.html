Sole trader expenses: what you can claim, in plain English

2026-07-10 · Finrock team

Every allowable expense reduces your taxable profit, so claiming properly is the easiest legitimate way to cut your tax bill. The test HMRC applies is simple to state: the cost must be wholly and exclusively for the trade.

Commonly missed claims

  • Use of home: either the flat rate (£10–£26/month depending on hours) or a proportion of actual bills.
  • Mileage: 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles, 25p after — often better than claiming actual vehicle costs.
  • Phone and broadband: the business proportion of the bill.
  • Bank charges and interest on a business account, card fees on sales.
  • Training that updates existing skills for your current trade.

Frequently claimed, usually wrongly

  • Everyday clothing — even if you only wear it for work. (Protective gear and uniforms are fine.)
  • Commuting-style travel to a single regular workplace.
  • Client entertaining — never allowable, even when it clearly wins work.

The £1,000 trading allowance

If your gross trading income is under £1,000 you may not need to report at all. Above it, you choose: deduct the flat £1,000 allowance or actual expenses — not both. If your real costs are higher, claim the real costs.

Keep the evidence

You must keep records for at least five years after the filing deadline. A photo of the receipt, captured at the till, is enough — provided it's stored somewhere you can retrieve it.
